\begin{abstract}
Predicting finishing outcomes in Formula 1 and optimizing combinatorial fantasy portfolios represents a complex challenge characterized by non-stationary competitor capabilities, non-linear scoring rules, extreme asymmetric tail risk, and impending 2026 regulatory changes. In this monograph, we present the mathematical architecture and empirical validation of the open-source F1 Prediction Engine. The system integrates: (1)~a numerically hardened Glicko-2 dynamic skill rating system with bounded volatility root-finding and constructor uncertainty floors; (2)~a 56-feature machine learning stacking ensemble coupling Random Forest regression with gradient-boosted pairwise rankers (XGBoost, LightGBM) via a novel dual-polarity rank normalization transformation; (3)~an econometric fuel-corrected tire degradation model isolating true wear gradients ($\lambda_{\text{fuel}} = 0.035$~s/lap) from practice telemetry; (4)~an empirical Bayes point calibration framework that eliminates optimistic zero-inflation bias under severe DNF penalties ($-15$ pts); (5)~a consistency-scaled stochastic Monte Carlo simulation engine featuring midfield Safety Car jump clamping; and (6)~a Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) portfolio optimizer enforcing the 2026 two-asset constructor cap across an expanded 22-car grid. Walk-forward backtesting across multi-season datasets demonstrates a Race MAE of 2.55 positions, a Spearman rank correlation of $\rho = +0.854$, 100\% winner prediction accuracy, and monotonic information gains across weekend operational stages.
\end{abstract}
